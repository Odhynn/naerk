Athalaar is a misty and mysterious landmass lying to the northwest of the continental Old World.

## History
The earliest records of Athalaar originate from the prehistoric times when the elves first walked upon Naerk. "Athalaar" in their language roughly translates to "moon storm", drawing inspiration perhaps from the unpredictable weather and the high tides.
There had been a draconic name for these foggy lands, which was unfortunately not preserved in the limited literary sources of that age, and the few dragons who remember it still are silent on the matter.

### Eltirion
It was the elven sages and mages of old that first investigated Athalaar's peculiar ties with the moon, namely the older one of the globe's satellites known as [[Qusm]] or Cosmodrome.
It is so far unknown when exactly Eltirion (translating to "Tower of Stars") was constructed in the highest mountains at the center of the continent, but a decent estimation would be between -2000 to -1600.
The observatory-palatial complex set up there by the elves acted as a projection focus point to the Cosmodrome in such a way that it functioned as a gateway to other worlds.

### The Maenhir
The ancient elves of Selanaar had found a way to tap into the raw terran magic of the [[Leylines]], and channel the power of the nodes through great standing stones on the surface.
Even before the Elven period began in earnest, many thousands of these _maenhir_ were erected across Athalaar and all the way outward into the continental shelf.

### Selanaar
As millennia passed, in Eltirion's shadow developed the civilization of [[Selanaar]].

### The Harrowing
![[Harrowing]]

Behind the eternally stormy coastal barrier that sailors from the outside hence called the **Shrieking Curtain**, certain regions survived the disaster and lived on, protected by the Menhirs that could hold the Harrowmist at bay. The size of such regions was (and is still) dependent on the state of their adjacent Menhir network. They can be as large as the entire eastern side of the continent (comprised mostly by Bjåglant), or as small as the fortified goblin villages on the Selanaari coasts (protected by a single menhir usually carved in the likeness of Angaeradh by the goblins[^1]).

As the centuries passed, Angaeradh and her servitors were able to gradually integrate more of the _maenhir_ into warding arrays that would cut off the spread of the Harrowmist, and reinforce the Ethereal veil in the liberated land beyond those lines. This endless task contracted the Harrowlands bit by bit, allowing nature to slowly reclaim the once haunted terrain, followed by civilisation. 

### Surviving isolated cultures

#### Kwarix
In the Kwarissian peninsula, protected by _"Hangradh's Wall"_ (a double array of a thousand menhirs spanning across a 150 miles wide isthmus) the dominant population of gnomes survived the disaster, and persevered for centuries surrounded by haunted lands and impassable seas, creating the state of [[Kwarix]] where they have continued to progress ever since, inheriting the most advanced secrets of Selanaar. Presently, Kwarix is synonymous with research in clockwork technology to the few scholars around the world who know of it.

#### Oni Territories
On the fringes of the Harrowland one may be unlucky enough to come upon the Oni. These lumbering, brutal humanoids were originally tutelary nature spirits (_"kami"_ in Sennarii) that suffered a terrible trauma when their sacred wards were warped by the Harrowing.
They now prowl along the mountainous regions that straddle the tenuous border between the Harrowlands and the reclaimed regions. 

#### Athar Tribes
Another ethnic group that managed to eke out a living in the newly haunted continent throughout the centuries were the [[Athar]] orc tribes, huddled around protective circles and _maenhir_ in the eastern woodlands and hills under the leadership of their shamans.[^2]
Their fierce fighting spirit and cunning knowledge of the land has seen them through many centuries of western incursions by Oni armies and eastern raids by Bjåglander settlers.

#### Bjåglant
The barbarian settlers to the northeast proved to be surprisingly resilient group as well. Part of the migrating refugee peoples of Thimvert, cut off from the rest of the world and after centuries of fighting against the elements, oni, ogres and orcs, they consolidated their hold over the land they called [[Bjåglant]].

### Fall of the Shrieking Curtain
Over the course of the 48th century the ring of eternal storm around the shores of Athalaar broke off and gradually receded. With the seas now tranversable, the Bjåglanders didn't take long to rediscover their ancestors' maritime ferocity, and set upon the neighbouring lands and islands on ships both raider and trader.

### Arrival of the Sennarii
In 4890, the [[Sennarin|Sennarii]] clans, refugees of a disaster in the Realms Beyond, appeared through the ancient elven cosmic portals in the now eponymous region, where they established themselves and expanded after many years of conflict with the Bjåglanders as well as the creatures that the Bjåglanders and the Athar had already been fighting off for the previous 1200 years.
The art and culture of these people is eerily similar to that of old Selanaar. Hypotheses outlining the possible influence of Selanaar to (or from) the Realms Beyond are increasingly alluded to in light of the recent [[Selanaari expeditions|expeditions]] of the Sennarii into the [[Harrowlands|haunted heartland]] of the ancient kingdom.

[^1]: The confidential research document "Survey of the _Menhir_ network in the Eastern Selanaari Peninsula" ([[Aileen|Rosewood]] and [[Lindiwe|Ap Lwgh]], 4818; access restricted by Sennarii authorities) has noted the existence of standing stones among possible archaeological sites scouted during the [[Selanaari Expeditions#Second Expedition, 30-54 Leafdrop 4816|4816 Sennari expedition in Selanaar]], that are similarly carved in a crude humanoid shape. The authors posit from magical evidence collected on the field that those were once _maenhir_ but were defaced post-Harrowing by goblins too ignorant to understand the ramifications of picking at the only thing keeping the horrors at bay. The _maenhir_ that did remain active after such reverent goblin art expression, still protect goblin villages in Harrowmist-free pockets within the Harrowlands to this day.

[^2]: Besides the goblins, association of the _maenhir_ with Angaeradh is also evident in the rest of the Athalaari cultures that survived the Harrowing, as indicated by linguistic evidence such as _"Hangradh's Wall"_ and _"Gowanëh:gotgö-Maniit"_ (Athar for "great witch spirit stone").
